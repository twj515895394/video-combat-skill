# Contact & Impact Physics

Use when hits, blocks or collisions feel weightless, fake or like choreographed posing.

This file defines **force transfer**, not gore.

## Universal impact chain

Every meaningful contact should be describable as:

`incoming mass/limb -> exact contact point -> local compression/deflection -> structural interruption -> center-of-mass reaction -> foot/balance consequence -> recovery/continuation`

A contact that does not visibly change anything is usually not a convincing hit.

## 1. Exact contact point

Never write only:
- hits him,
- punches his body,
- blocks the kick.

Specify the receiver point:
- upper sternum,
- left pectoral / upper chest,
- lower ribs,
- shoulder line,
- forearm radius/ulna area,
- outer thigh,
- shin,
- hip,
- upper back,
- wooden pillar / stone railing.

Also specify the striking surface when useful:
- palm heel,
- first two knuckles,
- forearm bone,
- elbow point/forearm edge,
- shoulder mass,
- heel,
- shin,
- sole.

## 2. Contact geometry

State the force direction:
- straight backward,
- diagonally down-and-back,
- outside-to-inside,
- upward through the torso,
- lateral across the guard,
- rotational across the shoulders.

The receiver's reaction must follow the same force vector.

## 3. Local deformation

At contact, show a short material/body response.

Torso hit:
- robe fabric dents/compresses,
- chest/abdomen folds slightly around the strike,
- shoulder line jolts,
- breath is visibly interrupted.

Forearm/shin collision:
- both limbs decelerate sharply,
- guard angle shifts,
- elbow/knee structure compresses,
- strike line is redirected rather than magically stopped.

Environment collision:
- wood flexes/splinters,
- plaster powders/cracks,
- stone chips/dusts slightly,
- railing vibrates or scrapes.

Do not exaggerate ordinary human impact into explosive VFX unless requested.

## 4. Structural interruption

A convincing strike changes body structure.

Examples:
- sternum palm -> shoulders recoil and spine inclines backward,
- rib/body hook -> torso folds/rotates toward the struck side,
- shoulder bump -> upper torso displaced while feet scramble to catch balance,
- forearm check -> attacking arm/leg line changes and the attacker's torso must compensate,
- low kick -> weight shifts off the struck leg or knee momentarily softens.

Avoid receiver remaining perfectly upright and unaffected.

## 5. Center-of-mass and foot consequence

Impact must reach the feet when force is meaningful.

Possible results:
- rear foot slides 10-30 cm,
- one recovery step catches the body,
- support heel lifts,
- stance narrows/widens,
- one knee bends to absorb force,
- receiver collides with wall/pillar/railing,
- receiver must post a hand to stop a fall.

Do not use huge knockback unless force and setup justify it.

## 6. Follow-through and recoil

Attacker also reacts to contact.

Examples:
- punching shoulder/hip decelerates at contact,
- palm heel remains compressed for a fraction before releasing,
- kicker's support leg absorbs the collision,
- elbow/shoulder strike carries body mass through the target line,
- blocked strike rebounds or is redirected into the next motion.

Avoid limbs passing through bodies or stopping with no recoil.

## 7. Impact levels

### Light contact / probe
Use:
- small redirection,
- guard shift,
- slight head/torso movement.

### Solid strike
Use:
- visible local compression,
- shoulder/hip/spine reaction,
- weight transfer to rear/side foot,
- short recovery step.

### Heavy decisive impact
Use sparingly:
- larger posture break,
- forced multi-step recovery,
- environment collision,
- hand post / knee drop,
only if the action setup supports it.

## 8. Defensive collision

A block/check is still a collision.

For a forearm check:
`incoming limb accelerates -> forearms/shin meet -> both decelerate -> defender's structure absorbs/redirects -> attacker line changes -> one side gains the next opening`

Do not write “blocks cleanly” with zero physical consequence.

## 9. Near-miss

Near-miss should also have physical information:
- sleeve snaps from air displacement,
- robe shifts,
- defender torso recoils away from line,
- attacker's missed limb overextends/lands and changes balance.

## 10. Sound synchronization

Sound must match material and timing.

Examples:
- palm/fist to clothed torso: short dense body thud + fabric slap,
- forearm-to-forearm: dry hard contact,
- shin/forearm: sharper bone/cloth contact,
- shoulder into body: deeper body thump,
- boot on stone: hard sole impact + scrape,
- body into wood: dull structural thud + wood vibration/splinter.

No impact sound before visual contact.

## 11. Prompt compression rule

Not every light touch needs a paragraph.

For fast exchanges:
- minor checks: contact point + line change,
- solid hits: contact point + local compression + posture/foot consequence,
- signature/heavy hits: full impact chain.

## 12. AI failure modes

- fist stops centimeters before target,
- limbs visually pass through torso,
- receiver reacts before contact,
- receiver stays rigid after a supposedly hard hit,
- hit causes generic backward glide with no foot mechanics,
- every hit causes identical reaction,
- impact sound is unsynchronized,
- block looks like two actors tapping limbs together.
