# Screen Direction & Continuity for Combat

Action cinema may use aggressive angles without losing orientation.

## Base relation

Establish:
- Fighter A screen side,
- Fighter B screen side,
- attack direction,
- key environment anchors.

Maintain until a visible crossing occurs.

## Camera side

Keep camera on a consistent observation side during a continuous exchange unless:
- visible movement crosses the relation,
- occlusion motivates a re-establishment,
- a new shot clearly re-maps space.

## Partial-subject shots

If only fist/leg/torso appears:
the motion direction must remain compatible with established relation.

Example:
A is screen-left attacking toward screen-right.
A fist close-up should still travel broadly left-to-right unless shot is explicitly POV/subjective.

## POV exception

POV can temporarily flatten screen direction.

Before/after POV:
re-establish with a relation or result shot.

## Crossing event

A true side swap needs:
- visible pass/cross,
- defender response,
- both fighters reorient,
- next shot confirms new positions.

No hidden swap under random cut.

## Height continuity

If fighter jumps:
next shot inherits:
- upward/downward phase,
- facing,
- trajectory,
- landing destination.

## Impact continuity

If close-up shows contact:
next shot cannot show receiver reacting in opposite direction.

## Environment continuity

Pillars, stairs, railings and broken objects provide orientation anchors.

Use them deliberately.
