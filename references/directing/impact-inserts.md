# Impact Inserts

Impact inserts isolate the contact mechanism and physical result.

They are not mandatory for every hit.

A good impact insert must show **where the force lands and what changes because of it**.

## II-01 Fist / Palm to Torso

Frame:
contact point from shoulder-to-waist region.

Show in order:
1. strike entering frame,
2. exact receiving point (upper sternum, pectoral line, lower ribs, shoulder line, abdomen),
3. cloth/body compression at contact,
4. receiver's chest/shoulder/torso structure changing in the same force direction,
5. weight consequence beginning to travel toward the feet.

Attacker:
- wrist/forearm alignment remains readable,
- shoulder/hip visibly decelerates into contact rather than passing through.

Receiver examples:
- sternum palm -> shoulders recoil + spine inclines back + rear foot catches weight,
- rib/body strike -> torso folds/rotates toward struck side + stance changes,
- shoulder-line strike -> upper torso yaws and lead/rear foot adjusts.

Faces optional.

Avoid:
- fist already touching chest when shot starts,
- chest remaining rigid after a supposedly solid hit,
- generic whole-body backward glide without local compression.

## II-02 Forearm Block / Check

Frame:
both forearms or shin+forearm.

Show:
- incoming limb direction,
- exact bone/forearm/shin receiving line,
- sudden contact deceleration,
- slight structural compression in both limbs,
- attack line redirected or guard angle changed,
- exit direction that creates the next opening.

Sound:
short dry contact, not metal clang.

A block should never look like two actors gently tapping limbs together.

## II-03 Elbow / Shoulder Collision

Use close-medium framing so body mass remains understandable.

Shoulder bump:
- show planted support,
- shoulder mass enters upper chest/shoulder girdle,
- receiving torso is displaced,
- feet scramble or recovery step catches center of mass.

Elbow:
- show short path and genuine close range,
- exact receiving surface,
- receiver head/guard/torso changes structure at contact.

## II-04 Kick Impact

For decisive kick:
1. wider proof of support and trajectory,
2. optional tighter contact insert,
3. result shot.

Contact insert should show:
- heel/shin/sole meeting exact receiving line,
- receiver's guard/torso/leg compressing or shifting,
- kicking leg decelerating or redirecting,
- support leg absorbing collision.

Do not make contact insert replace the entire kick.

## II-05 Body-to-Environment Impact

Frame may isolate:
- shoulder hitting pillar,
- back hitting wall,
- foot crushing bench edge,
- hand catching railing.

Show:
body arrival -> exact material contact -> material response -> body compression/recoil -> recovery consequence.

Material examples:
- wood flexes/splinters,
- plaster powders/cracks,
- stone chips/dusts slightly,
- railing vibrates/scrapes.

## II-06 Guard Compression

Close shot of:
incoming strike,
forearms/guard compressing toward face/chest,
receiver's elbows/shoulders shifting,
stance or torso absorbing the force.

The compressed guard should create a believable next opening.

## II-07 Miss / Near-Miss Insert

No contact is still information.

Show:
- fist grazing sleeve,
- kick crossing inches from ribs,
- robe displaced by passing limb,
- hair/cloth air response,
- attacker's limb overextending or landing into a changed stance.

Miss must create continuation state.

## Impact hierarchy

Minor contact:
small guard/line change only.

Solid contact:
local compression + posture change + foot/balance consequence.

Signature/heavy impact:
full force-transfer chain + stronger recovery/environment result, used selectively.

Do not make every contact equally heavy.

## Camera behavior

At contact:
- camera may perform one short controlled jolt,
- do not maintain continuous shake,
- do not zoom randomly,
- camera jolt must not replace actual body reaction.

## Editing

Impact insert works best when:
approach is proven before cut,
contact becomes the cut anchor,
result is inherited after cut.

Never use editing to hide missing physical contact.
