# New-Wave Hong Kong Wuxia — Tsui Hark Reference Profile

This profile distills documented characteristics associated with Tsui Hark's wuxia/action work into reusable directing mechanisms.

Do not copy a specific film scene shot-for-shot.
Do not output only “in the style of Tsui Hark.”
Translate into abstract camera, staging, rhythm and production-design language.

## Documented reference basis

Useful source observations include:
- BFI describes Tsui as a leading Hong Kong New Wave filmmaker who pushed wuxia into a lavish new era and highlights the furious kineticism, expressive lighting, free-wheeling camera and inventive POV of *The Blade*.
- Criterion's *Once Upon a Time in China* essay emphasizes musicality of movement, grounded martial-arts realism in some sequences, and Yuen-family creativity with tempo, props, sets, and attack-defense balance.
- Sammo Hung has described Tsui as having a strong sense of the martial-arts world and the ability to present details and the spirit of Chinese martial arts/swordplay onscreen, alongside strong art direction.

## Profile DNA

### 1. Movement feels larger than the technique
A punch, kick, leap or weapon line may trigger:
- camera reframe,
- foreground motion,
- environment reaction,
- verticality,
- prop movement,
- cut rhythm.

The fight exists inside a kinetic world, not on an empty stage.

### 2. Space changes constantly
Characters may move:
- floor -> stairs,
- corridor -> pillar line,
- table/railing -> landing zone,
- foreground -> depth,
- high -> low.

But every change needs a physical path.

### 3. Environment becomes choreography
Props and set geometry create:
- obstacles,
- rebound surfaces,
- elevation,
- visual wipes,
- attack lanes,
- destruction.

### 4. Camera may become expressive
Allowed:
- fast lateral reframe,
- whip-pan,
- low/high angle shift,
- subjective/near-subjective attack insert,
- abrupt detail shot,
- aggressive landing intercept.

Constraint:
important body mechanics still need a readable proof angle.

### 5. Shot subjects can fragment
A shot may focus only on:
- foot plant,
- robe/sleeve,
- fist approaching lens,
- forearm collision,
- chest impact,
- broken railing,
- eyes snapping to threat,
- landing feet.

This fragmentation should intensify, not confuse.

### 6. Rhythm is musical, not metronomic
Use:
- brief spatial setup,
- sudden explosive burst,
- fast information changes,
- short result hold,
- immediate continuation.

Do not mechanically cut every second.

### 7. Art direction participates
Lighting, costume movement, smoke, dust, fabric, architecture and props help create motion vectors.

## Useful shot grammar

### TH-01 Detail ignition
Open or interrupt with a physical detail:
foot plant / sleeve snap / hand catching rail / eye turn.

Then expand to full action.

### TH-02 Expressive POV threat
Attack moves toward lens or near defender-eye axis.
Follow with block/near-miss/result.

Use once, not repeatedly.

### TH-03 Foreground chaos / readable core
Sleeves, props or bodies cross foreground while the important attack line remains visible deeper in frame.

### TH-04 Environment escalation
Fight changes location because pressure physically drives characters into stairs, pillars, benches, ladders, rails or doors.

### TH-05 Vertical burst
One subject changes height via visible support.
Camera reframes vertically or intercepts landing.
Avoid weightless float unless fantasy level requires it.

### TH-06 Impact fragmentation
A decisive exchange may be represented as:
full-body approach -> contact detail -> environmental/body result.

### TH-07 Kinetic exit
End the segment while movement is still propagating:
pursuit continues,
fighter exits frame,
landing leads to next attack,
debris is still falling.

## For unarmed wuxia

Combine with:
- actual Chinese base style,
- grounded-wuxia,
- qinggong only when needed,
- range or recovery combination grammar.

Do not let “new-wave wuxia” erase:
- support foot,
- facing,
- body weight,
- attack-defense causality.

## AI failure risks

- camera energy replaces real choreography,
- every shot becomes Dutch/POV/whip-pan,
- bodies teleport under occlusion,
- both fighters jump together,
- props appear spontaneously,
- fragmented inserts lose screen direction.

## Prompt translation vocabulary

Use combinations such as:
- kinetic new-wave Hong Kong wuxia language,
- expressive low/high reframing,
- aggressive but motivated whip-pan,
- fragmented impact insert,
- prop-driven spatial choreography,
- foreground occlusion with preserved action continuity,
- fast depth changes,
- dramatic architecture-driven movement,
- musical action rhythm,
- readable full-body proof between expressive inserts.
