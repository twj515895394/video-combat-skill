# King Hu — Rhythmic Widescreen Wuxia Reference

This profile translates documented King Hu formal traits into reusable directing mechanisms.

## Source basis

Criterion describes *Dragon Inn* through rhythmic editing, meticulous choreography, widescreen composition and fluid camera movement.
David Bordwell's Criterion essay on *A Touch of Zen* highlights:
- long lateral tracking,
- abstract/balletic composition,
- percussion-like rhythm,
- very short inserts,
- sudden long shots,
- jump-like spatial compression,
- editing that can stretch or pinch time.

Sources:
- https://www.criterion.com/films/28790
- https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/5787-dragon-inn-poised-for-battle
- https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/4141-a-touch-of-%20flying

## Profile DNA

### 1. Widescreen spatial geometry
Bodies are arranged as moving shapes within architecture/landscape.

### 2. Rhythm before constant camera motion
Editing, entrances, exits, eyelines and percussion-like action beats can create speed even when camera is restrained.

### 3. Long shot vs abrupt detail contrast
A distant spatial frame may suddenly give way to:
- eyes,
- weapon/hand,
- foot,
- attack arrival,
then return wide.

### 4. Sudden entrances
Characters may appear from:
- frame edges,
- background depth,
- behind architecture,
provided space was established.

### 5. Controlled stillness -> explosive motion
Tension may build through watchfulness, then release in a short violent burst.

## Useful shot grammar

KH-01 wide spatial map -> sudden edge entrance.
KH-02 lateral tracking through multiple action nodes.
KH-03 gaze/detail insert -> immediate attack.
KH-04 staccato micro-cuts around a very fast exchange.
KH-05 sudden distant long shot after close fragmentation.
KH-06 architecture as frame-within-frame.

## AI use

Best for:
- ensemble wuxia,
- inns/courtyards/bamboo/architecture,
- spatial ambush,
- elegant but sharp combat.

Avoid:
- making every shot hyperactive,
- losing body positions during rhythmic cuts.
