# Sammo Hung — Practical Kinetic Action Reference

Use as an analytical profile for practical Hong Kong action: body weight, stunt reality, spatial positioning and strong physical consequences.

## Source basis

BFI's Sammo Hung interview emphasizes the practical-risk tradition of Hong Kong action and Hung's recollection that King Hu taught performers how to move into optimal positions for the camera. Hung also stresses the martial-arts world/detail and visual sensibility he valued in Tsui Hark.

Source:
https://www.bfi.org.uk/interviews/sammo-hung

## Profile DNA

### 1. Bodies feel heavy
Falls, impacts, trips and recoveries carry inertia.

### 2. Stunt action has visible cause
Furniture breaks because body weight reaches it.
Falls travel along understandable paths.

### 3. Performer positioning serves camera
Blocking creates readable:
- silhouettes,
- impact lines,
- entrances,
- exits.

### 4. Wide proof + strong impact
The audience often sees enough body to believe the stunt before a tighter result.

### 5. Recovery is part of action
Getting up, stumbling, grabbing support and re-entering are useful beats.

## Useful grammar

SH-01 wide stunt proof -> weighted impact.
SH-02 obstacle collision -> practical destruction -> recovery.
SH-03 fall/roll -> hand catches surface -> immediate return.
SH-04 pressure drives fighter through set geometry.
SH-05 body-size/weight differences influence choreography.

## Camera

Prefer:

- restrained handheld,
- close result insert only after physical path is proven.
