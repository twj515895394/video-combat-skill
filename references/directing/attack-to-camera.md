# Attack Toward Camera / Threat Lens

Use when the attack should feel as if it enters the viewer's space.

This is not a replacement for a readable side/full-body proof shot.

## ATC-01 Fist Toward Lens

Setup:
attacker begins at short-to-mid range in three-quarter orientation.

Action:
fist travels toward a point near lens axis, increasing rapidly in apparent size.

Camera:
fixed or tiny backward recoil.

Opponent:
may be off-axis, partially visible, or represented by defensive forearm entering frame.

Risk:
AI may interpret this as punching the camera instead of opponent.

Constraint:
establish opponent's line immediately before or keep defender limb/torso edge visible.

## ATC-02 Kick Past Lens

A foot or shin passes close to lens without literal camera collision.

Useful:
- round kick,
- side kick,
- sweep passing foreground.

Keep:
support/trajectory established in preceding or same shot.

## ATC-03 Defender-Eye Threat

Camera approximates defender's eye/shoulder line.

Attacker's strike approaches frame.
Defender's forearm/hand enters foreground to intercept.

This can show:
threat -> defense -> contact
without both full bodies.

## ATC-04 Near-Miss Lens Wipe

Attack passes so close that glove-free fist, sleeve, leg or robe briefly fills frame.

Use as motivated transition into:
- impact result,
- new angle,
- defender counter.

## Rules

- Do not use repeated attacks-to-lens; it loses clarity.
- One perspective threat shot per short sequence is usually enough.
- Maintain screen direction from previous relation shot.
- Never let POV-style framing create unexplained position swap.
