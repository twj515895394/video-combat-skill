# Action Shot Pattern Library

Reusable shot cards for fight direction. These are visual modules, not mandatory coverage.

Each shot card has one primary information job.

## SP-01 Relation Two-Shot

Purpose:
establish both fighters, screen sides, distance and attack direction.

Framing:
medium-wide / full-body.

Use:
opening of exchange, after major position change, after POV/detail burst.

Tempo rule:
if both fighters are actively exchanging complete techniques, keep the movement fast and continuous. This is not a slow instructional frame.

Do not:
hold this framing for the entire fight.

## SP-02 Single Attacker Three-Quarter

Purpose:
show attack preparation, acceleration, body mechanics and forward pressure.

Frame:
attacker dominates 60-80% of frame.
Defender may appear only as shoulder/arm/edge or remain just outside frame.

Useful:
punch chain, kick chamber, qinggong push-off, pursuit initiation.

This shot allows a fast attack to begin before cutting to contact/defense.

## SP-03 Single Defender Reaction

Purpose:
show defense/recovery without forcing attacker fully into frame.

Attacker may be represented by:
- fist entering edge,
- leg crossing foreground,
- sleeve,
- shadow/motion cue,
- established off-screen attack direction.

Useful:
slip, forearm check, stumble, wall catch, boot skid, balance recovery.

## SP-04 Attack Toward Lens

Purpose:
subjective threat.

Frame:
fist/foot grows rapidly toward lens axis.

Constraint:
opponent line must already be understood or defender limb remains visible.

Use sparingly.

## SP-05 Fist / Palm-to-Chest Contact Insert

Purpose:
prove decisive torso impact without slowing the whole exchange.

Frame:
hand/forearm + receiver chest/shoulder region.

Show:
approach -> contact -> cloth compression -> structural recoil.

Faces optional.

## SP-06 Forearm / Guard Collision Insert

Purpose:
show block/check/redirection.

Frame:
incoming limb and defensive forearm dominate.

Show:
collision, sudden deceleration, compression/redirection and exit direction.

## SP-07 Foot Pivot Insert

Purpose:
prove biomechanical support.

Frame:
boots/feet and lower legs.

Use:
round kick, hook, body turn, throw entry.

Can briefly emphasize technique detail while the surrounding fight remains fast.

## SP-08 Push-Off Insert

Purpose:
prove qinggong/jump support.

Frame:
foot plants on floor/pillar/railing.

Show:
surface contact -> knee compression -> extension.

Next shot:
trajectory proof.

## SP-09 Low Sweep Ground Shot

Purpose:
show sweep line and defender foot response.

Camera:
near floor, lateral to sweep.

Defender:
foot lifts/steps; no synchronized jump.

## SP-10 Over-Shoulder Threat

Purpose:
preserve opponent relationship while making attack feel close.

Foreground:
defender shoulder/head edge.

Background/mid:
attacker advances.

Useful:
straight punch, palm, elbow approach.

## SP-11 Defender-Eye / Near-POV Block

Purpose:
attack enters viewer space then is intercepted.

Foreground:
defender hand/forearm rises.

Mid:
attacking fist/foot.

Do not use as literal camera hit.

## SP-12 Sleeve / Robe Wipe

Purpose:
motivated cut/reframe.

A moving sleeve or robe fills frame for a fraction.

Next angle inherits same body state.

## SP-13 Pillar Occlusion Reveal

Purpose:
use architecture for spatial tension.

One fighter temporarily hidden by pillar.
Movement reveals attack/escape.

No teleport behind pillar.

## SP-14 Landing Intercept

Purpose:
make airborne movement readable without chasing wildly.

Camera waits at landing area.
Subject enters frame and lands.

Show:
first foot -> knee absorption -> continuation.

## SP-15 Impact Result Shot

Purpose:
show consequence instead of repeating the strike.

Examples:
- receiver stumbles,
- shoulder hits pillar,
- body slides across bench,
- foot catches balance,
- defender's torso folds around the hit.

Attacker may be partly or fully off-screen if direction is established.

## SP-16 Environment Damage Insert

Purpose:
prove force and route change.

Subject:
splintering wood, cracked plaster, sliding table, dust burst.

Use only after physical contact is established.

## SP-17 Recovery Detail

Purpose:
show failure/recovery becoming next action.

Examples:
- hand catches floor,
- boot skids,
- palm grabs railing,
- knee compresses after landing.

Next beat grows directly from recovery.

## SP-18 Eye-Line Trigger

Purpose:
show target recognition / incoming threat.

Close:
eyes/head snap toward off-screen motion.

Use:
before sudden attack direction change.

Do not overuse facial inserts in a pure mechanics exchange.

## SP-19 Depth Rush

Purpose:
show attacker moving from background to foreground.

Camera:
mostly fixed or slight retreat.

Use:
long corridor, courtyard, stair depth.

Keeps direction clear while increasing urgency.

## SP-20 Exit / Re-entry

Purpose:
allow one fighter to leave frame during fast displacement.

Rule:
screen direction remains consistent.
Re-entry must follow known route.

Useful:
wuxia chase,
pillar/doorway movement,
fast pursuit.

## SP-21 Attack-Handoff Cut

Purpose:
prevent slow turn-taking.

Pattern:
- shot A ends while Fighter A's attack is still resolving,
- cut to Fighter B already converting the defense/recovery into counter-initiation,
- no neutral reset between shots.

Useful:
parry-to-counter, missed kick-to-entry, impact retreat-to-pursuit.

## SP-22 Single-Subject Launch -> Contact Cut

Purpose:
let one fighter own the frame during attack initiation.

Pattern:
- attacker-only medium/full shot shows acceleration/chamber,
- cut on limb extension,
- next shot shows contact/block/near-miss detail,
- opponent need not be fully visible in the first shot.

## SP-23 Defender-Only Recovery -> Re-entry

Purpose:
show consequence while keeping fight momentum.

Pattern:
- attacker leaves or remains outside frame,
- defender skids/stumbles/posts/lands,
- next threat enters frame before defender returns to neutral stance.

## SP-24 Foreground Limb Barrage

Purpose:
create speed, threat and depth without requiring the attacker to remain fully visible.

Composition:
- defender stays readable in mid-ground,
- attacker's fist, forearm, shin, foot or sleeve repeatedly enters from a known frame edge,
- the attacking limb may dominate the foreground and briefly grow large near lens,
- attacker's full body can remain partial/off-screen as long as attack direction is already established.

Choreography use:
- rapid kick barrage,
- repeated straight/hook entries,
- defender-only body evasion and redirection,
- one fighter under sustained pressure.

Defender behavior:
- torso/head may leave the attack line before the hand/forearm makes guiding contact,
- each incoming limb should create a new threat line rather than repeat the same pose,
- defense remains continuous; no reset between attacks.

Why it works:
- foreground angular speed makes attacks feel faster,
- defender mechanics stay visually readable,
- the fight avoids static two-person master-shot staging.

Continuity constraints:
- preserve the established attack side,
- do not let the off-screen attacker teleport around the defender,
- occasional relation shot must re-anchor geography after a major spatial change.

AI failure risks:
- disembodied extra limbs,
- attack direction changing between frames,
- foreground limb contacting camera instead of defender,
- repeated identical kicks with no body-state progression.

## Selection rule

A short sequence does not need every pattern.

Usually select only the patterns needed to create:
- one spatial anchor,
- fast complete-body exchange,
- one or more single-subject initiations/reactions,
- selected contact/details,
- result/recovery,
- re-establishment only when geography changes.

The same pattern should not repeat without a new information purpose.

Reject overreliance on SP-01. A cinematic fight should not be a static master shot with occasional zooms.
