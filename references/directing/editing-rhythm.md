# Action Editing Rhythm

Editing rhythm should preserve causal action.

## ER-01 Proof -> Compression -> Result

A reliable action pattern:

1. Proof shot:
show enough body to understand attack.

2. Compression:
optional closer angle at contact/block.

3. Result:
show displacement, landing, broken environment or new range.

Do not use all three for every hit.

## ER-02 Long Take for Mechanics

Use longer continuous shot when:
- footwork relationship matters,
- complex throw must be proved,
- both fighters change range repeatedly,
- choreography itself is the spectacle.

Camera should stay restrained.

## ER-03 Fast Cut for Information Change

Cut faster when:
- different body detail becomes important,
- attack suddenly changes direction/height,
- environment creates new threat,
- subjective danger moment is useful.

Fast editing is not permission to skip cause/effect.

## ER-04 Staccato Burst

Several short shots can compress a very fast exchange:
- eyes / feet / attack / block / impact / result.

Use only if spatial anchor already exists.

## ER-05 Rhythm Contrast

Action can feel faster by contrast:
brief controlled setup -> explosive burst -> short physical recovery -> immediate new burst.

“Recovery” means body physics, not posing.

## ER-06 Cut on Motion

Good cut anchors:
- punch extension,
- kick crossing frame,
- sleeve wipe,
- body falling,
- hand grabbing,
- foot landing,
- head snapping toward threat.

## ER-07 Cut on Impact

Cut precisely at contact to switch from attack line to receiver result.

Avoid:
impact shown twice from two angles unless user explicitly wants replay/slow motion.

## AI-generation caution

AI video often handles fewer coherent shots better than hyperfragmentation.
For 10 seconds:
use only the number of cuts required to improve readability.
Do not chase “cinematic” by adding 10 unrelated angles.
