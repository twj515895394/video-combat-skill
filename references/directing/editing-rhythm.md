# Action Editing Rhythm

Editing rhythm should preserve causal action and use cinematography to control perceived speed.

## Core rule — slow the observation, not the duel

When both fighters are substantially visible and performing complete attack-defense movement, keep the choreography fast, continuous and real-time.

If one moment needs to feel slower or more readable, change the shot:
- isolate the contact,
- isolate the support foot,
- isolate the defender reaction,
- isolate the environment result.

Do not make two full-body fighters slowly take turns merely to improve readability.

## ER-01 Proof -> Compression -> Result

A reliable action pattern:

1. Proof shot:
show enough body to understand attack and spatial relation.

2. Compression:
optional tighter angle at contact/block/footwork detail.

3. Result:
show displacement, landing, broken environment or new range.

Do not use all three for every hit.

## ER-02 Full-body two-shot = active tempo

A wide / medium-wide two-shot is for:
- route,
- footwork,
- style contrast,
- kick/throw readability,
- continuous exchange.

Tempo expectation:
**fast and connected**.

Avoid:
`A attacks -> pause -> B defends -> pause -> B attacks -> A defends -> reset.`

Prefer overlap:
- defense already loads counter,
- miss becomes forced landing,
- impact retreat becomes pursuit,
- recovery step becomes next attack base.

## ER-03 Detail shot = emphasis without slowing both fighters

Close/insert shots can temporarily make a key event easier to read because less information occupies the frame.

Good subjects:
- fist/palm entering torso,
- forearm-shin collision,
- wrist control,
- boot skid,
- support-foot pivot,
- pillar/railing contact,
- local body recoil.

The action itself remains real-time by default.

## ER-04 Long Take for Mechanics

Use longer continuous shot when:
- footwork relationship matters,
- complex throw must be proved,
- both fighters change range repeatedly,
- choreography itself is the spectacle.

But a long take must remain active. Do not turn it into slow instructional demonstration.

## ER-05 Fast Cut for Information Change

Cut faster when:
- a different body detail becomes important,
- attack suddenly changes direction/height,
- environment creates new threat,
- subjective danger moment is useful,
- one fighter exits/re-enters frame.

Fast editing is not permission to skip cause/effect.

## ER-06 Staccato Burst

Several short shots can compress a very fast exchange:
- attacker initiation,
- footwork,
- block/contact,
- receiving point,
- result/recovery.

Not every shot needs both fighters.
Use only if spatial anchor already exists.

## ER-07 Rhythm Contrast

Action can feel faster by contrast:
brief spatial setup -> explosive two-person exchange -> contact/detail emphasis -> immediate continuation.

“Recovery” means body physics, not posing.

## ER-08 Cut on Motion

Good cut anchors:
- punch extension,
- kick crossing frame,
- sleeve wipe,
- body falling,
- hand grabbing,
- foot landing,
- head snapping toward threat,
- shoulder entering wall/pillar.

## ER-09 Cut on Impact

Cut precisely at contact to switch from attack line to receiver/result information.

Avoid showing the same impact twice from two angles unless replay/slow-motion is explicitly requested.

## ER-10 Real slow motion

Default: no slow motion.

If explicit slow motion is requested, prefer one selected detail:
- decisive contact,
- foot plant/push-off,
- near miss,
- environment break,
- receiving-body reaction.

Avoid slowing an entire wide two-person exchange unless the user explicitly requests stylized slow-motion choreography.

## AI-generation caution

AI video often handles coherent motivated cuts better than decorative hyperfragmentation.
Use only the cuts that add new action information.

Reject a clip that is mostly one static master two-shot with slow turn-taking, even if the actions themselves are technically correct.
