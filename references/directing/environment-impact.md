# Environment Impact & Destruction

Environment is a physical participant.

Use only when geometry has been established.

## EI-01 Wall / Pillar Collision

Approach:
fighter is driven toward known surface.

Contact:
specific body region reaches material.

Material response:
- wood: flex, splinter, dust/chips,
- plaster: crack, powder,
- stone: chip, dust, small fracture,
- metal: dent/ring,
depending force.

Body result:
recoil, compression, slide or support against surface.

## EI-02 Table / Bench Break

Show support structure and weight direction.

Sequence:
body/object loads surface -> structure bends/breaks -> fragments continue with inertia -> fighter lands/recover.

Do not explode furniture before contact.

## EI-03 Railing Interaction

Can be:
- hand catch,
- foot plant,
- stumble stop,
- vault,
- body collision.

Keep height and location consistent across shots.

## EI-04 Floor Response

Useful:
- dust under hard plant,
- shoe skid,
- small debris displacement,
- water splash,
- fabric drag.

Do not overuse dramatic cracks for ordinary human force.

## EI-05 Hanging / Loose Objects

Curtains, lanterns, papers, bamboo blinds, hanging cloth may:
- react to air displacement,
- briefly occlude frame,
- reveal movement direction.

They create motion cues without needing extra VFX.

## Damage continuity

Once material breaks:
- fragments remain,
- gap/crack remains,
- dust can settle gradually.

Do not restore environment between shots.

## Camera

Damage can have its own short insert if it:
- proves force,
- changes route,
- creates next obstacle,
- motivates cut.

Otherwise keep it within the action shot.
