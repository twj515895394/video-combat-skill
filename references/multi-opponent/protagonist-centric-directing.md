# Protagonist-Centric Multi-Opponent Directing

The protagonist is the visual anchor of one-vs-many action.

## Primary rule

Camera organization follows:
1. protagonist body and movement,
2. current attacker,
3. next incoming threat,
4. environment lane.

Do not distribute visual attention equally across all enemies.

## Subject hierarchy

### Tier 1 — Protagonist
Must remain readable enough to understand:
- current movement direction,
- balance / recovery,
- current target,
- immediate threat response.

### Tier 2 — Active attacker
Must be readable when attack mechanics matter.

### Tier 3 — Next attacker / supporting enemies
May appear only as:
- arm entering frame edge,
- shoulder behind pillar,
- partial torso in background,
- leg sweeping into foreground,
- silhouette approaching through doorway,
- hand reaching from off-axis.

They do not need full-body or face coverage before becoming active.

## Shot grammar

### MP-CAM-01 Anchor Follow
Medium / medium-wide camera follows protagonist movement through the group.
Used during complex scheduling.

### MP-CAM-02 Edge Threat
Current action remains centered on protagonist while next attacker appears at edge of frame.
This visually prepares the target handoff.

### MP-CAM-03 Foreground Intrusion
A new arm/leg/body crosses foreground before becoming full attacker.
Use to create pressure without showing everyone fully.

### MP-CAM-04 Partial Occlusion Threat
Pillar, doorway, furniture or another body partially hides an approaching enemy.
Reveal occurs through motion, not teleportation.

### MP-CAM-05 Local Impact Insert
Briefly isolate:
- protagonist forearm blocking one strike,
- protagonist shoulder hitting wall,
- enemy fist contacting torso,
- protagonist palm/foot displacing one enemy.
Then return to protagonist spatial context.

### MP-CAM-06 Space Rebuild
After large route change, use medium-wide / wide frame or motivated camera move to re-establish:
- protagonist,
- active threat,
- remaining attack lanes.

## One-take behavior

If using a long take:
- camera follows protagonist route,
- reframes when target changes,
- does not orbit the entire group,
- allows enemies to enter/exit edges organically.

## Cut behavior

If cutting is allowed, cut by:
- action paragraph,
- target handoff,
- route change,
- major impact,
- environment state change.

Do not cut for every punch.

## Face rule

Not every enemy needs a face shot.
Not every protagonist beat needs a face shot.

Useful non-face subjects:
- footwork,
- forearm collision,
- torso impact,
- shoulder glance against wall,
- hand catching table edge,
- incoming fist from off-screen,
- enemy leg entering foreground.

## AI failure modes

- camera abandons protagonist to show random enemy,
- all enemies are forced into a clean group composition,
- supporting attackers disappear between frames,
- constant wide shot makes action flat,
- constant close-up destroys group geography,
- target switch happens without protagonist head/body reorientation.
