# Recovery, Reentry & Off-Screen Threat Sound

Multi-opponent pressure depends on enemies remaining part of the scene even when temporarily inactive.

## Enemy recovery states

An enemy may be:
- staggered but standing,
- pushed behind obstacle,
- kneeling / catching balance,
- getting up,
- circling around blocked lane,
- temporarily down,
- moving back into approach position.

Do not treat every successful hit as permanent elimination.

## Reentry rule

A recovering enemy may return only through a physically plausible route.

Good:
- enemy knocked behind table reappears after moving around table edge,
- enemy who fell near wall rises in background and approaches again,
- enemy displaced to screen-left later reenters from that same spatial side after visible/aural setup.

Bad:
- enemy disappears after hit and suddenly appears behind protagonist.

## Protagonist failure / damage

The protagonist is not required to remain untouched.

Valid states:
- gets clipped,
- loses balance,
- is briefly grabbed,
- bumps wall/pillar,
- fails an attack,
- must use hand on table/rail to recover.

That failure must become the next state.

Example:
protagonist is struck in shoulder -> torso turns and foot steps sideways to catch balance -> new attacker enters that exposed side -> protagonist uses wall/table support to pivot away.

## Breathing / perception beat

Very short beats can include:
- one sharp breath,
- head snap toward new threat,
- quick eye-line shift,
- shoe skid,
- hand catching support.

These beats preserve realism and clarify threat handoff.
They must not become a neutral pose reset.

## Off-screen threat sound

Sound is a major part of multi-opponent staging.

Useful off-screen cues:
- running footsteps approaching from behind/side,
- cloth movement,
- shoe scrape,
- short breath/grunt,
- body hitting furniture,
- enemy getting back up,
- hand contacting wall/table,
- object displaced by approaching body.

These sounds can announce a threat before full visual reveal.

## Sound hierarchy

1. protagonist-local body sound,
2. current attack/contact,
3. next approaching threat,
4. recovering/background enemies,
5. environment ambience.

## Sync rule

Every visible:
- hit,
- miss whoosh,
- footstep,
- cloth snap,
- support contact,
- wall/pillar/table collision,
- breath/grunt
should match the exact physical moment.

Avoid exaggerated superhero booms for ordinary human impacts.

## AI failure modes

- every enemy hit produces full knockout,
- recovering enemies vanish,
- off-screen sounds imply enemies from impossible directions,
- constant shouting masks action cues,
- sound effects happen before physical contact.
