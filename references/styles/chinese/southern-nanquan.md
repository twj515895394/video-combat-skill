# Southern Nanquan — Choreography Model

This is a broad cinematic/choreographic model, not a claim that all southern schools move identically.

## Style DNA
- Lower, stable base.
- Compact hand actions dominate.
- Short power and forearm/bridge contact are visually important.
- Less emphasis on long aerial travel than northern long-fist language.
- Close pressure should feel rooted rather than bouncy.

## Range Model
mid-close -> bridge/contact -> short strike.
Entry uses small steps, pressure and line occupation.
Exit often remains close enough to continue rather than bouncing far away.

## Action Cards

### NQ-BRIDGE-CHECK
Incoming arm meets defender forearm at short range.
Forearm pressure redirects rather than freezing the attack.
Exit: contact line remains useful for palm/elbow entry.

### NQ-SHORT-PALM
Rear or lead foot compresses into floor; hips advance minimally; palm heel travels short direct line.
Result: receiver's torso shifts/recoils rather than flying away.
Exit: striker remains rooted and opponent-facing.

### NQ-LOW-KICK
Compact low-line kick without large airborne chamber.
Purpose: disturb stance while hands occupy upper line.

### NQ-FOREARM-ENTRY
Defender uses forearm contact to open or compress guard, then steps only enough to enter short striking range.

## Combination Grammar
- forearm check -> line remains connected -> short palm.
- low kick alters base -> bridge hand occupies arm -> elbow/palm.
- opponent retreats -> short advancing step maintains pressure.

## Tactical Failure Conditions
- very long kicking distance,
- excessive jumping,
- large spin chains,
- feet bouncing without grounded pressure.

## Camera Proof
Chest-to-knees for bridge work; full-body proof when stance shift or low kick matters.

## Prompt Vocabulary
rooted low stance, compact forearm bridge, short palm heel, close-range elbow, small advancing step, stable base.
