# Bajiquan — Choreography Model

## Style DNA
- Short explosive entry.
- Whole body arrives with the strike.
- Elbows, shoulders and forearms are visually important.
- Power should look connected to floor and step, not arm-only.
- Best used as “crash through range” choreography.

## Action Cards

### BJ-STOMP-ENTRY
Lead/rear step lands with deliberate weight transfer, closing range.
Do not exaggerate into supernatural ground shock.

### BJ-ELBOW-ENTRY
Forearm/guard clears line; body steps close; elbow travels short path.
Impact changes opponent posture and distance.

### BJ-SHOULDER-BUMP
Requires chest/shoulder proximity.
Support foot plants; hips/body drive shoulder into upper torso.
Receiver shifts/stumbles due to center-of-mass displacement.

### BJ-FOREARM-CRASH
Forearm makes structural contact with guard/upper arm to open line.
Immediately feeds elbow/palm/shoulder.

## Combination Grammar
- guard contact -> forceful step -> forearm crash -> elbow.
- opponent backs up -> planted step -> shoulder bump continues pursuit.
- failed long strike by opponent -> Baji fighter enters during recovery.

## Tactical Failure Conditions
- no room to plant feet,
- target stays safely outside entry range,
- arm-only depiction loses style identity.

## Camera Proof
Three-quarter full/medium body angle showing floor connection and torso displacement.

## Prompt Vocabulary
explosive planted step, short forearm crash, close elbow, shoulder-driven body check, rooted whole-body impact.
