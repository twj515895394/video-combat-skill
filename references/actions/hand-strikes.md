# Atomic Hand / Upper-Limb Strikes

These are biomechanical primitives. Style files decide when/how they are used.

## HS-LEAD-STRAIGHT

### Start
- lead side slightly forward,
- lead hand near guard,
- rear hand remains protective,
- knees soft and base stable.

### Execution
- lead foot / stance pressure initiates subtly,
- lead shoulder advances behind the hand,
- fist travels on a direct line,
- pelvis/torso contribution stays compact rather than over-rotating.

### Peak / contact
- wrist stays aligned behind striking surface,
- elbow remains structurally connected to shoulder,
- rear hand stays available.

### Recovery / exit
- hand retracts or becomes a frame depending style,
- stance remains usable for cross / angle / retreat.

AI risk:
arm-only jab with frozen torso or hand detached from shoulder line.

## HS-REAR-STRAIGHT

### Start
- rear hand at guard,
- lead foot establishes front side,
- rear leg/hip available to drive.

### Load / execution
- rear foot pressure contributes,
- rear hip rotates forward,
- rear shoulder follows hip,
- fist travels straight toward target,
- lead hand remains guard/counterbalance.

### Peak
- shoulder and hip are rotated enough to support the line,
- rear heel may release/pivot depending style,
- torso does not overreach past the support base.

### Recovery
- rear hand retracts or becomes frame,
- lead side becomes naturally available for hook / angle / defense.

Miss:
weight/shoulder may become extended; that state must feed next action.

## HS-HOOK

### Start
- target is close enough for an arc rather than a long swing,
- lead/rear side is explicitly chosen,
- elbow begins flexed.

### Load
- knees remain soft,
- hip and torso rotate from support base,
- shoulder follows the torso.

### Peak / contact
- fist and elbow travel on one coherent arc,
- elbow does not flare into an anatomically detached windmill,
- wrist remains aligned,
- head stays opponent-aware,
- opposite hand remains guard.

### Exit
- rotation either recoils or continues into the next physically compatible action.

AI risk:
arm-only swing, elbow too far behind hand, excessive full-body spin.

## HS-UPPERCUT

### Start
- genuine close range,
- knees/hips slightly compressed,
- striking hand near torso rather than dropped far behind.

### Execution
- legs and hips rise/drive subtly,
- fist travels on a short upward-forward path,
- shoulder remains connected to torso.

### Peak
- elbow stays below/behind fist in a plausible line,
- torso does not launch vertically or hyperextend.

### Exit
- body remains compact for hook, frame or retreat.

## HS-PALM-HEEL

### Start
- palm aligned with forearm,
- fingers relaxed/controlled and not folded unnaturally,
- base supports forward pressure.

### Execution
- floor/hip/torso contribute according to style,
- palm heel travels short/direct into torso/head line,
- shoulder remains structurally connected.

### Contact
- palm heel, not loose fingertips, is the force surface,
- wrist remains stable,
- opposite hand remains guard/control.

### Exit
- striking arm can retract, frame or remain in bridge contact.

Useful for Chinese martial arts/wuxia.

## HS-FOREARM-STRIKE

### Start
- close range is already established,
- elbow flexed enough to keep forearm structurally connected.

### Execution
- torso/step carries the forearm through a short path,
- forearm bone/outer structure becomes contact surface.

### Exit
- forearm remains useful for frame, bridge, elbow or release.

Must have genuine proximity.

## HS-HORIZONTAL-ELBOW

### Start
- close range,
- elbow chambered without exaggerated wind-up,
- non-striking hand guards or controls.

### Execution
- hip/torso rotate through a short arc,
- elbow point travels horizontally toward target,
- shoulder stays connected to body mass.

### Exit
- rotation stops in a usable close-range stance.

AI risk:
wide arm swing that looks like a hook with bent wrist.

## HS-DOWNWARD-ELBOW

### Start
- arm rises only enough to create the line,
- base remains grounded.

### Execution
- body weight and shoulder contribute to a short downward path,
- elbow point remains the striking surface.

Avoid giant theatrical overhead wind-up unless the cinematic layer explicitly requires it.

## HS-SHOULDER-BUMP

### Start
- chest/shoulder proximity already exists,
- support foot is planted,
- knees/hips remain under center of mass.

### Load / execution
- hips and torso drive together,
- shoulder mass enters the receiver rather than the arm alone,
- head remains off the direct collision line.

### Contact
- shoulder/upper arm transfers body mass into upper torso,
- support leg and rear/other leg stabilize the drive.

### Exit
- attacker keeps balance for frame, follow-up strike or separation.

Receiver displacement should be modest and weighty, not explosive flight.
