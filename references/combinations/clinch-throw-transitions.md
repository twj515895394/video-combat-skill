# Clinch / Throw Transitions

Purpose: make standing grappling emerge from visible contact instead of appearing magically.

## CT-01 Striking Collision -> Forearm Contact -> Body Lock

Trigger:
two short strikes/defenses compress range until forearms/shoulders touch.

Changed state:
space for long strikes disappears.

Transition:
one fighter gains inside arm/torso position and connects body lock.

Next:
trip, wall pressure, turn, or separation depending style.

## CT-02 Kick Catch -> Upper-Body Control -> Support Removal

Trigger:
kick is caught.

Changed state:
kicker is one-leg balanced and usually frames/hops.

Transition:
catcher adds shoulder/wrist/body contact to control posture.

Finish:
sweep/reap the remaining support or turn the upper body.

## CT-03 Failed Punch -> Arm Contact -> Standing Throw

Trigger:
punch is parried/covered and arm remains reachable.

Changed state:
wrist/upper arm/garment contact becomes available.

Transition:
thrower steps close, establishes second contact, then off-balances.

Rule:
do not jump directly from parry to airborne throw.

## CT-04 Clinch Knee Threat -> Turn / Trip

Trigger:
close frame/clinch posture.

Changed state:
one fighter narrows/raises stance to knee or defend knee.

Transition:
opponent changes upper-body direction while blocking/reaping foot.

Useful:
Muay Thai-inspired cinematic clinch, Sanda/Shuai Jiao hybrids.

## CT-05 Failed Throw -> Direction Change

Trigger:
opponent steps out or widens base against first throw.

Changed state:
weight shifts onto a new leg.

Continuation:
attacker maintains contact and attacks the newly loaded support direction.

This is better than resetting grips.

## CT-06 Throw Landing -> Immediate Positional Result

Trigger:
receiver lands.

Changed state:
attacker still has momentum/contact.

Continuation:
- disengage and re-engage for cinematic standing fight,
- settle top control for MMA/BJJ/Sambo,
- remain standing if style/rules demand.

Always define who retains balance.

## AI risks
- receiver levitates before foot/hip action,
- limbs merge,
- throw happens off-screen,
- both bodies rotate identically like synchronized dance.
